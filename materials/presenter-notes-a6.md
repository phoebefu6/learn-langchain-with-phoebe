## Never-cut beats

1. The Klarna arc, told whole - headline first, reversal second, in that order (the emotional core of the session; if time collapses, cut everything else first)
2. The scorecard build with gamed twins named out loud - the deliverable the whole track has been walking toward
3. Kill criteria ("we stop if ...") - without this beat, the scorecard is decoration and every project stays unfalsifiable
4. The closing handoff (a4 matrix + a5 score + a6 scorecard = the governance file) - it is what makes six sessions feel like one artifact

## Q&A landmines

- "So did Klarna's agent fail or succeed?" - Neither word survives contact with the data: the efficiency numbers largely held, the "replace everyone" narrative did not, and the company runs a deliberate hybrid now. The refusal to give a one-word answer IS the lesson - metrics, not verdicts.
- "Are these case-study numbers trustworthy?" - Partially, and that is the point: Klarna/Uber/AppFolio/LinkedIn figures are company- or vendor-published - real but curated. That is why the page flags them and why Part 2 teaches the three filters. Model the honesty you are asking for.
- "95% of pilots show no P&L impact - why are we doing any of this?" - Carry the caveat (definitions vary, samples skew), then flip it: the same research shows bought/partnered and honestly-measured deployments succeed at far higher rates. The statistic is an argument for the scorecard, not against agents.
- "Won't kill criteria demoralize the team?" - The opposite, in practice: pre-committed stop conditions protect teams from zombie projects and protect careers from being chained to them. A project that can only be declared a success is the demoralizing kind.
- "What happens after this session?" - Two paths: hand your governance file (a4+a5+a6) to your data team as the brief, and/or continue into the builder track (b1) if you want to see the machinery. Neither requires the other - say that plainly, it is the track's promise kept.
