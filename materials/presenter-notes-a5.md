## Never-cut beats

1. The crossover chart with the room placing their own use case against ~1M/yr - it converts opinion into arithmetic in real time
2. The run-rate line: "the MVP is the cheap part" - the single most budget-saving sentence in the track
3. The MIT 2x WITH its caveat and its translation (reason + team + evals) - the stat alone without the translation just starts fights
4. The vendor-model question - it is the negotiating tool they will use within a month

## Cuts if long

- The minimal-build-team card (marked self-study - point at it, homework covers it)
- The "pitch that flipped" real-world callout - summarize in one sentence if needed
- Exercise step 5 (drafting the vendor question) - it survives as homework
- The negotiation corollary callout (exit portability) - one sentence version: "ask what leaves with you"

## Q&A landmines

- "So should WE build or buy?" - Refuse the general question, gladly: the door is chosen per use case. Offer the two-input answer: which side of ~1M is your volume, and is the use case commodity or differentiating? If they name a real case, run those two inputs live - it is the best possible demo.
- "Are those cost numbers accurate for us?" - They are 2025-2026 market ranges, honest as ranges and wrong as quotes; the shape of the decision (fixed-plus-tokens vs per-conversation) is the durable part. Your team plugs in your numbers - that is data-team question 3.
- "Doesn't the MIT stat mean internal builds are irresponsible?" - No: it moves the burden of proof, it does not ban. Uber-style differentiated builds with real teams are exactly the survivors. Also carry the methodology caveat honestly - it strengthens your credibility with quants in the room.
- "Our engineers say vendor products are just wrappers we could build in a weekend." - Often true of the demo, never true of the product: the wrapper is the operations, evals, compliance, and support. That IS the vendor business model beat - point back at it.
- "If the framework is free, why does this course teach a vendor's stack at all?" - Because the free layer is where your team's skills compound and your exit options live; the paid layer is a separate, negotiable decision. Free framework + eyes-open vendor choice is the whole thesis of the session.
